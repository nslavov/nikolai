<p class="intro"><span class="dropcap">M</span>uch of what surrounds us is imperfect or even deeply flawed. The flaws may breed resignation and cynicism. Instead, they should motivate us:  Let's combine creativity to imagine what is possible, resourcefulness to make it feasible, openness to learn from those who disagree, and inspirational <a href="https://nikolai.slavovlab.net/Research,-mentoring,-and-teaching">mentorship</a> to propel an autocatalytic chain reaction.
</p>

### Openness and courage
Setting a positive example often requires the courage to pursue ideas that challenge prevailing assumptions and to persevere in the face of opposition. Persistence while [ignoring criticism](https://x.com/slavov_n/status/2090822247095173265?s=20) is not a virtue:  It often reflects ignorant stubbornness that leads to failure. Perseverance takes serious note of critical arguments and evaluates them to understand what they get right and what they get wrong. It means listening carefully enough to understand why someone holds a different view, examining the assumptions behind the disagreement, and remaining willing to change our minds when evidence demands it.

This intellectual openness should guide how we pursue ambitious science. It is easy to propose a far-reaching idea that lies well beyond current understanding. It is far harder to find a genuinely new approach that makes such ambitious ideas testable with the tools, time, and resources available. This requires creativity and [resourcefulness](https://nikolai.slavovlab.net/Resourcefulness/). An ambitious idea becomes scientifically powerful not when it is difficult to imagine or impossible to refute, but when we can devise a way to confront it with reality and make it [feasible](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02805-w).

That is also where intellectual leadership matters. Instead of complaining that science, institutions, or scientific discourse should be better, we should contribute to improving them. We can demonstrate a higher standard through the work we do and the way we do it. We can pursue unconventional questions, explain our reasoning clearly, engage seriously with opposing arguments, and build experiments that distinguish between attractive possibilities and the truth.



### Intellectual multiplier
Achieving ambitious goals requires both intellectual inspiration and a culture of high standards. It requires inspiring students and colleagues to see possibilities they might not otherwise consider, and giving them the intellectual freedom and support to pursue those possibilities. Ideas become more powerful as they are challenged, refined, and extended by team. This not only contributes to greater success of the scientific undertakings, but also supports the intellectual growth of the team. It is tremendously rewarding to see students becoming increasingly independent thinkers.

This is one of the measures of scientific and academic success that matters most to me: our intellectual heritage reflected in the growth of our students and colleagues. My proudest moments are not our most influential research articles, but watching members of my teams become independent scientists who carry forward not just techniques, but a way of asking and answering questions. The initial spark becomes a brilliant light illuminating new corners if truths.


We cannot control whether every institution around us reaches the standard we would like. But we can choose the example we set: to think ambitiously without confusing speculation with knowledge, to listen seriously without abandoning judgment, to test ideas rather than merely defend them, and to inspire others to carry those ideas further than we could ourselves.


**Raise the bar and help your team reach it.**
